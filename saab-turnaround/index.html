We cut the workforce to fit three car lines, which took 10.8M a period out of engineering cost

Too many people for the volume

1,114 engineers and 800 factory workers served cars selling under 4,000 units a period.

Cut in three steps, as cash allowed

210 factory jobs in Round 2, 600 engineers in Round 3 and 334 in Round 5. One-off cost 122M.

Kept what the cars need

180 engineers stay on the Sedan and Wagon. Factory labour ran at 88% utilisation in Round 5.

Bottom line. Engineering cost fell 84% and the saving repeats every period.

We raised prices twice, by up to 10% each time, and volume kept growing

Prices were too low

Buyers in Sweden and Europe barely reacted to price. We raised by up to 10% in Round 2.

It worked, so we did it again

Volume held, so we raised again in Round 3. Sedan and Wagon list prices are up 21%.

North America paid the bill

Sedan sales there fell from 796 to 657. Other markets grew far more than that loss.

Bottom line. Revenue per car rose 26% while units sold rose 57%.

We moved marketing to the Wagon, our most profitable car, and it became half of all sales

The Wagon earns the most per car

It carries the highest price and margin, so each extra Wagon is worth about two Sedans.

Marketing followed the margin

Wagon marketing rose from 2.4M to 6.2M a period. Sedan spend in North America was cut.

The Convertible came last

Lowest volume and no engineers assigned. We kept it on sale with minimal spend.

Bottom line. Wagon sales grew 79% and reached 49% of volume.

We managed cash week by week, so the restructuring never needed emergency funding

A weekly cash forecast from day one

Every large decision was timed against the week it would hit the bank account.

Suppliers as short term credit, then repaid

Supplier arrears peaked at 92M to fund severance and are down to 67M.

One planned bridge loan

30M covered the single period when customer receipts paused.

Bottom line. 122M of restructuring was paid from operations, with no asset sale and no new equity.

We declined every disposal and equity offer, because each one worked against our strategy

Seven offers in 24 weeks

Engines, markets, assets and equity. Each one traded future earnings for cash today.

One test for all of them

Does it help the strategy, or only this month?

The last one was the hardest

245M for 53.9% valued all of Saab at 455M.

Bottom line. Such deals suit a company short of cash or shrinking. We were neither, so we held firm.

Together these decisions lifted EBITDA every period, to 61.9M in the last one

Price fixed the margin first

Rounds 2 and 3 took EBITDA from a loss to 27.8M.

Volume, mix and cost did the rest

Since Round 3, more cars added 19M, margin per car 9M and lower costs 8M.

The company is intact

Plant, Parts and Accessories, the UK importer and 100% of the equity remain.

Bottom line. At this run rate Saab earns about 800M of EBITDA a year.

Saab is profitable, self funded and worth an estimated 6.1B to 6.9B, and the next steps are clear

What we achieved

EBITDA of 61.9M a period, no emergency funding, no disposal and no dilution.

What we learned

Keep the factory loaded. Price where buyers stay and back it with marketing. Fix the business before selling equity.

What comes next

Settle supplier arrears. Rebuild Sedan stock. Restart the 9-1 from earnings. Enter China through distribution.

Bottom line. We ask the board to endorse the roadmap and keep the equity until the price reflects the new earnings.
